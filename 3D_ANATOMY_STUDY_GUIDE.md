# 3D Anatomy Study Guide: Trunk and Thoracic Relationships

This guide suggests anatomical structures and spatial relationships that can be explored using the accompanying 3D Slicer scene. Use the 3D models together with the original CT slices, dissector, lectures, and gross anatomy laboratory materials.

> **Study reminder:** This is an optional supplemental reference. Examination content and expectations are determined by the syllabus and officially designated course materials. Segmentations are approximations and may contain errors; they should not replace study of the original images and course resources. **See [README](https://github.com/chz31/anatomy_education_resources/blob/main/README.md) for full educational notice, data source, license, and attribution notes**.

## Before you begin
- **Tutorial video for loading data into Slicer and interactively viewing 3D anatomy:** [video link](https://youtu.be/gJbVeTes4bc)
- Use the **eye icon** in the Data or Segmentations panel to show or hide individual structures.
- Rotate, pan, and zoom the 3D view to examine relationships from multiple directions.
- Adjust segment opacity when one structure obscures another or simply turn off the **eye icon** to hide it.
- Compare the 3D surfaces with axial, sagittal, and coronal CT slices.
- When useful, display a 2D slice plane in the 3D view and move it through the region of interest.
- Do not assume every groove, impression, vessel, or boundary has been segmented perfectly. Verify what you see against the CT and course materials.

## 1. Trunk skeleton

In the segmentation named `trunk_and_upper_limb_bones_etc`, identify:

- Cervical, thoracic, and lumbar vertebrae
- Sacrum
- Scapulae
- Clavicles
- True ribs and false ribs
- Sternum
- Costal cartilages

For individual vertebrae and ribs, expand the `Total` segmentation and display the relevant segments one at a time.

### Relationships to examine

- How the thoracic vertebrae, ribs, costal cartilages, and sternum form the thoracic cage
- The positions of the clavicles and scapulae relative to the rib cage
- The transition from the thoracic spine to the lumbar spine

## 2. Lungs: surfaces, lobes, and fissures

Display the right and left lungs and verify that:

- The right lung has three lobes: superior, middle, and inferior.
- The left lung has two lobes: superior and inferior.
- The right lung has horizontal and oblique fissures.
- The left lung has an oblique fissure.

Identify on each lung:

- Apex
- Costal surface
- Mediastinal surface
- Diaphragmatic surface (base)

<img width="400" alt="Screenshot 2026-09-01 154535" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/57b6d4bc-6ee7-49e2-9dee-82db26e11404" />


### Relationships to examine

- How the costal surfaces follow the curvature of the thoracic wall
- How the bases of the lungs relate to the diaphragm
- How the mediastinal surfaces face the heart and other mediastinal structures

## 3. Lung impressions and mediastinal relationships

Turn related structures on and off, and make the lungs partially transparent when helpful.

On the **right lung**, examine or approximate the positions of:

- Cardiac impression
- Esophageal groove
- Groove or impression related to the inferior vena cava
- Superior vena cava

**The azygos vein is not present in this segmentation, and the groove for the superior vena cava may not be clearly defined on the lung surface. Displaying the superior vena cava can help you approximate its relationship to the right lung.**

## 4. Relationships among major blood vessels

Hide the lungs, then display vessels individually or in small groups. Following the dissector and course materials, identify:

- Superior vena cava and inferior vena cava
- Right and left brachiocephalic veins
- Pulmonary trunk and right and left pulmonary arteries
- Pulmonary veins
- Aortic arch and descending aorta
- Brachiocephalic trunk
- Right and left common carotid arteries
- Right and left subclavian arteries

### Relationships to examine

- Formation of the superior vena cava by the brachiocephalic veins
- Position of the pulmonary trunk and pulmonary arteries relative to the ascending aorta and superior vena cava
- How the right common carotid and right subclavian arteries arise from the brachiocephalic trunk, in contrast with the direct origins of the left common carotid and left subclavian arteries from the aortic arch
- Entry of the superior and inferior venae cavae into the right atrium
- Entry of the pulmonary veins into the left atrium

Use several viewing angles; a relationship that appears unclear in an anterior view may become obvious from a superior, posterior, or lateral view.

## 5. Hilum and root of each lung

Use **both the 3D view and 2D CT slices**. Examine the relative positions of:

- Main bronchus
- Pulmonary artery
- Superior and inferior pulmonary veins

Make the lung transparent or hide it by turning off the **eye icon**. A sagittal CT slice displayed within the 3D view can be especially helpful when moving through the hilum.

### Questions to consider

- How are the bronchus, pulmonary artery, and pulmonary veins arranged from anterior to posterior at each lung root?
- How does the superior-inferior relationship between the pulmonary artery and main bronchus differ on the right and left?
- How might the aortic arch contribute to the different arrangement on the left?
- Can you confirm the 3D relationship in more than one CT plane?

## Final self-check

Before finishing, confirm that you can:

- Identify the major skeletal structures of the thoracic cage.
- Distinguish the lobes and fissures of the right and left lungs.
- Relate each lung's mediastinal surface to the heart, great vessels, and esophagus.
- Trace the major arteries and veins through the superior mediastinum and heart.
- Compare the organization of the right and left lung roots using both 3D surfaces and CT slices.

Return to the dissector, lectures, laboratory work, and syllabus to determine the level of detail required for the course and examinations.
